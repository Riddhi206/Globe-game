# THE SIGNAL: a daily geography thriller told in text messages

Concept pitch, 5 October 2026. The current story mode, The Unmaking, stays as it is.

**Status:** Act 1 (7 episodes) is built as a pilot behind the `signal` switch: `signal-data.js`, `signal.js`, `pin.js`, `supabase/015_signal.sql`. Changes from this pitch: the heroine is Meera Joshi (Delhi), her sister Tara; Pin Drop (find a city) instead of country guesses; choices change trust, pins and later scenes, with 3 Act 1 endings; the world's split is shown next episode, but the "world majority decides canon" vote is not built yet (planned for the Act 3 finale). The "why can't she see where she is" gap is answered by a locked company phone and partial views, and becomes the Act 2 twist: the person texting was never Meera.

## The one-line pitch

**A missing journalist texts you photos from wherever she's being held, and only you can work out where in the world she is. One episode a day. Everyone plays the same one. The world decides what she does next.**

It's *Lifeline* (survival told in texts) crossed with *GeoGuessr* (where in the world is this?), released on Wordle's daily rhythm, with the cliffhangers of a streaming thriller.

## Why this, and why now

The Unmaking is a fantasy told *around* the guessing game. Its story and its play are two separate things. The Signal makes **the story the guessing**: every clue is a plot beat, and every correct guess saves a person you care about.

What it borrows, and why each piece works:

| From | What it does for us |
|---|---|
| Chat fiction (Hooked, Lifeline, Episode) | The fastest way into a story on a phone. Reads like your own messages, costs almost nothing to produce, and is huge with mass audiences. |
| Daily drops (Wordle, Spelling Bee) | An appointment every day. Everyone talks about *today's* episode, and there's no binge-and-forget. |
| Cliffhangers (streaming thrillers) | Each episode ends on a question. The next one unlocks tomorrow. |
| Community choice (Telltale's "62% chose…", Twitch Plays) | The world votes, the story follows the majority, and you find out tomorrow whether you were with them. |
| Geography puzzles (GeoGuessr, Carmen Sandiego) | Our core game: hot and cold guesses on a globe, now with a reason to care. |

## The story (Season 1, 21 episodes over 3 weeks)

**Mira Okafor**, a 29-year-old investigative journalist, was looking into a shipping company that moves things it shouldn't. She stops answering her editor. Then *your* phone buzzes. She has your number because you were the last person to reply to her viral post about maps.

> **Mira:** I don't have long. They took my phone, this one's a burner.
> **Mira:** I don't know where I am. I'm sending you everything I can see.
> **Mira:** 📷 *[clue card: a sign in a script you don't recognise, a white minibus, a mountain on the horizon]*
> **Mira:** please. where am I?

### Three acts

- **Act 1, the escape (episodes 1–7).** Mira is moved from place to place. You find her each day, and she gets a step ahead. Easy clues: famous landmarks, big flags, unique scripts. You learn to trust each other.
- **Midpoint twist (episode 10).** A second number starts texting you, claiming to be *the real Mira*, with clues that contradict the first. Which one is lying? The world votes. Both versions get written. The vote picks which one becomes canon.
- **Act 2, the hunt (episodes 11–17).** Now *you* are hunting the people moving her. The clues get subtle: which side of the road cars drive on, plug sockets, sun angle, the currency on a receipt, the language of a radio broadcast.
- **Act 3, the signal (episodes 18–21).** The finale is a **live event**: everyone plays at 8 PM on the last day, and a global vote decides how it ends. Mira's last message references the player's own city (from their time zone) and their streak, so it lands as personal.

Each player's choices are remembered, so Mira's tone towards you changes ("You told me to run in episode 4. You were right."). The plot itself follows the world's majority, so everyone shares one canon story to talk about. That's the Telltale trick: personal *and* communal.

## One episode, minute by minute (5–7 minutes)

1. **"Previously on The Signal"** (10 s): three bubbles recapping yesterday, including the world's vote result: *"62% told Mira to run. She ran."*
2. **Messages arrive** (1–2 min) with typing indicators and short delays. They set the scene, stakes and character. Tap to speed up; nothing is skippable that holds a clue.
3. **The hunt** (2–4 min): Mira sends clue cards one at a time. You guess on the globe as usual (hot and cold colours, distance, arrows). **Each wrong guess unlocks the next clue**, so struggling players still finish, and sharp players feel brilliant finding her on clue 1.
4. **The pin drop** (10 s): the camera flies to her (the new end-of-game trail), and her photo card develops like a Polaroid. Your score is "clues needed", from 1 to 6.
5. **The choice** (20 s): two options and a 15-second timer, for example *"There's a back door / There's a window"*. Your pick is private until tomorrow.
6. **The cliffhanger** (10 s): one last message, then the screen cuts to black. *"Episode 8 drops tomorrow at 7 PM."* That lines up with the 7 PM notification already planned.
7. **The share card**: spoiler-free, *"I found Mira in 2 clues · Episode 7 · THE SIGNAL"*, with your clue squares. It's Wordle's viral loop.

## The hooks, and the line we won't cross

Every tactic below is used by today's biggest games and apps. Where a tactic exists to exploit people rather than delight them, we adopt the honest version.

| Hook | How The Signal uses it | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | Stakes in the first 10 seconds ("I don't have long") | None needed |
| Cliffhanger | Every episode ends mid-moment | No episode ever ends without a resolution of its own puzzle |
| Appointment cadence | A new episode daily at 7 PM | Missed episodes stay playable, with no punishment beyond the streak |
| Variable reward | Find her on clue 1 (rare and thrilling) or on clue 5 | The reward is pride, never paid boosts |
| Parasocial character | Mira remembers your name, your choices and your streak | Opt-in notifications only. Mira never guilt-trips ("I'll die if you don't play") |
| Social proof | "62% chose…", "41,203 found her today" | Real numbers only |
| FOMO | The live finale, season-limited badges | The finale replays afterwards for latecomers |
| Collection | An evidence board: a corkboard of every place you found her, linked with red string | None needed |
| Mystery box | Two Miras, hidden coordinates, a secret 22nd message | None needed |
| Identity | "Detective" titles, your city in the finale | The city comes from the time zone and stays on the device, as The Unmaking already does |
| Streak | Daily streak for episodes, protected by the planned streak freeze | None needed |
| Social play | Rooms get a **Story night**: play the day's episode together, see each other's choices | None needed |

**Never:** paying to skip waits, fake timers that punish, or notifications written to sound like an emergency in real life. Age-appropriate tension (PG-13 thriller, no gore), because the game is for all ages.

## What makes it award-worthy

- **A new form:** a daily, globally shared, community-steered thriller that teaches real geography without feeling like school. That's a strong pitch for the **Webby Awards** (Games and Web), **Games for Change** (learning), and **IGF**-style innovation categories.
- **Accessible by design:** text-first, so it works with screen readers once the globe gets text alternatives, adjustable text speed, reduced motion, and a colour-blind mode for the heat colours.
- **Cultural reach:** clues celebrate everyday life everywhere (street food, scripts, buses, music), not just landmarks. That makes it shareable across countries, with no single "home" market.

## Why it's cheap to build

- **No new art pipeline.** Chat bubbles, typing dots and clue cards are HTML/CSS. Clue "photos" are illustrated cards made from icons, scripts and colours, with no photo licensing. Mira is a silhouette and a voice.
- **The globe game already exists:** guessing, colours, the trail, sharing, streaks, notifications (planned), rooms and analytics.
- **Episodes are data,** like `story-data.js`: about 40 messages, 6 clues and 1 choice each. Twenty-one episodes is roughly a novella of writing, and both branches of each vote are written.
- **The server parts are small:** an episodes table (or static JSON plus release dates), a votes table with one function, and a community-results function. Same pattern as challenges.

## How we'd know it's working

Before building the full season, ship a **3-episode pilot** behind a feature switch (`signal`), to testers first, then 20% of players. Compare against The Unmaking using the new dashboard:

| Metric | Target for the pilot |
|---|---|
| Episode 1 completion (of players who open it) | 75%+ |
| Came back for episode 2 the next day | 45%+ |
| Voted in the choice | 70%+ |
| Shared the episode card | 15%+ |
| Turned on notifications | 25%+ |

It's also worth checking The Unmaking's own numbers first (chapter 1 finish rate, dialogue skips), so we know *which* part of the old story lost people.

## Alternatives considered

- **"The Last Flight"**: a plane goes missing, and passengers send messages from where it might have landed. Strong hook, but darker, and narrower once the plane is found.
- **"Atlas Academy"**: a cosy found-family story about a school for young cartographers. Gentler, broader age appeal, weaker cliffhangers.

The Signal wins on hook strength, shareability and daily rhythm.

## Decisions before building

1. **Tone:** thriller (The Signal), or cosy (Atlas Academy)?
2. **Cadence:** one episode a day for everyone, or let new players catch up up to 3 a day?
3. **Choices:** world majority decides canon (recommended), or each player branches privately?
4. **Name:** "The Signal", or something more Borderline-flavoured?
5. **Pilot first:** 3 episodes and measure, or commit to the full season?
